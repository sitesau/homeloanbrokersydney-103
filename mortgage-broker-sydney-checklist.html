Mortgage Broker Sydney Checklist Explained

Arriving unprepared limits what a broker can test in the appointment. The parent guide frames broker guidance around the blocker in front of the borrower, so this checklist turns that idea into a preparation routine. Before the conversation, write one short summary covering:

  • the goal: first purchase, refinance, investment, construction, renovation or pre-approval
  • the suburb or property type
  • the deposit position
  • the income pattern
  • existing debts
  • any timing pressure such as a contract deadline, auction plan or building milestone

Starting with the borrower situation rather than a rate table follows the approach of the Mortgage Broker Sydney Common Mistakes: 2026 Guide, which covers the errors worth sidestepping once the comparison begins.

Documents and numbers to have ready

Lenders assess borrowing power using the actual interest rate plus a 3 percentage point APRA buffer, and borrowing capacity can vary between lenders. The numbers supplied at the meeting shape the range of options a broker can realistically test. Prepare:

  • income details, stating whether income is PAYG, business, rental or mixed
  • expenses, existing debts and dependant details
  • the deposit amount, since a smaller deposit can affect lenders mortgage insurance and loan structure
  • the property context: suburb, dwelling type, or land and build plans

Self-employed borrowers should ask early which documents a lender may need for non-standard income, so the file is not stalled later in the process.

Questions that test the comparison logic

A broker review is broader than a single lender review because options can span major banks, smaller lenders and non-bank lenders. Disclosure belongs in the comparison, since Australian mortgage brokers work under a legal best interests duty regulated by ASIC and must disclose in writing how they are paid before a borrower proceeds. Questions worth putting on the list:

  1. Which lenders are being compared, and which policy issue does each option solve?
  2. Are fixed, variable or offset features relevant to this goal?
  3. How does lenders mortgage insurance affect the application at this deposit size?
  4. Is any borrower fee payable, given most home loan borrowers do not pay an out-of-pocket broker fee because the lender pays commission after settlement, while complex or commercial cases may involve a disclosed fee?

Checks for each borrower situation

The same preparation flexes by situation, which is where locality and timing enter. Greater Sydney borrowers across the Hills, the North Shore, the south-west and the Sutherland Shire face the same lender rules, but property type and contract context differ.

  • First home buyer: check deposit, pre-approval and first-home steps before making an offer.
  • Refinancer: state whether the review concerns repayments, equity release, rate switching or debt consolidation.
  • Investor: ask how borrowing power, equity release, rentvesting and structure are being assessed.
  • Construction or renovation: raise progressive-drawdown finance rather than a standard established-property loan.

A single lender review can still suit a borrower who already prefers that lender and appears to fit its rules. For the pitfalls that follow preparation, the guide to common broker mistakes in Sydney is the natural next read.

Pre-approval and what can change

Treat pre-approval as conditional. Provide the suburb or property type and the issue blocking the next step, then confirm which income, expense, debt and dependant details the lender will assess. Before committing, ask what could change before formal approval, settlement or drawdown, and compare fixed, variable and offset features only where they fit the goal. A broker review does not remove credit assessment, so borrowers still need to understand loan features, repayments and costs before proceeding, as ASIC Moneysmart advises. Commission context also belongs on the checklist: the parent guide gives broker commission ranges of roughly 0.6 to 0.7 percent upfront after settlement, and around 0.15 to 0.2 percent trail each year while the loan runs.

  1. Define the goal. State whether the loan is for a first purchase, refinance, investment, construction, renovation or pre-approval, and note the suburb or property type.
  2. Assemble the numbers. List income, expenses, debts, dependants, deposit and timing pressure so borrowing power can be tested across lender options.
  3. Request the comparison logic. Ask which lenders are being compared, the policy issue behind each recommendation, and whether fixed, variable or offset features fit the goal.
  4. Check disclosure and stress-test. Confirm any borrower fee and broker remuneration in writing, then ask what could change before formal approval, settlement or drawdown.

Common questions

Do I pay a mortgage broker directly for a home loan in Sydney? Most home loan borrowers do not pay an out-of-pocket broker fee, because the lender pays commission after settlement. Complex or commercial cases may involve a disclosed fee, and brokers must disclose how they are paid in writing before a borrower proceeds.

Why can my borrowing capacity differ between lenders? Lenders assess borrowing power using the actual interest rate plus a 3 percentage point APRA buffer, and each lender applies its own assessment, so capacity can vary between lenders even with the same borrower details.

Is pre-approval a guarantee of final loan approval? No. Pre-approval should be treated as conditional. Confirm which income, expense, debt and dependant details the lender will assess, and ask what could change before formal approval, settlement or drawdown.
